Forest change on Indonesia's five largest islands, from space

Real satellite mosaics of Sumatra, Borneo, Java, Sulawesi and Papua. Dark green is closed forest; lighter greens, browns and straight-edged blocks are plantations, farmland and cleared ground. Pick a year on the timeline under the map or press play, then zoom in to a province to see clearing spread. Switch on the land-use layer to colour forest, oil palm, cities and mines for the selected year.

Land-use classification

Click a class to hide or show it. Forest: tree cover in 2000 (≥30% canopy) not yet cleared by the selected year (Hansen/UMD). Oil palm and pulpwood: GFW Spatial Database of Planted Trees v2, mapped around 2020; a plantation pixel stays green until the year its forest was cleared. Pulpwood is shown separately so acacia plantations are not counted as forest. Cities & structures: Esri / Impact Observatory 10 m land cover for each year from 2017; the 2017 map stands in for earlier years. Mining: mine footprints mapped from about 2019 imagery (Maus et al. 2022). Unshaded land is other non-forest: farms, shrub, burnt or bare ground, water.

Plantation and mine outlines are fixed in time, so land that was already non-forest in 2000 shows its later use from the first year. The layer is computed in your browser from public map tiles and is coarse when zoomed out; zoom in for 30 m detail.

Imagery: 2000–2012 from the Global Land Survey Landsat mosaics (USGS/NASA, served by Esri), built from the clearest scenes around 2000, 2005 and 2010, so some cloud and haze remains. 2016–2025 from Sentinel-2 cloudless annual mosaics by EOX IT Services (contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data; 2017 onwards licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). No public cloud-free mosaic exists yet for 2026, so the last step shows 2025. Figures in the tables are tree cover (≥30% canopy, plantations included) from Hansen/UMD Global Forest Change v1.13 via Global Forest Watch. Borneo includes Sabah, Sarawak and Brunei; Papua is the Indonesian provinces of Papua and Papua Barat.
