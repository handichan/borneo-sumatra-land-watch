Key findings

Anger at officials travels furthest; corporate blame is constant but quieter

  1. Two waves, not one. Engagement peaked in mid–late August (Kalimantan haze, #SaveKalimantan, WALHI's concession data) and again from 8 October, when Palangka Raya turned red and posts about Japan's aid being halted went viral. The second wave is now the larger one: 12–18 Oct alone carries 1.5M engagement, more than twice the August peak, as haze reached Kuala Lumpur, the new capital IKN and, by 16 Oct, the skies over Jakarta.
  2. Governance outrage drives reach. The most-shared posts attack the President, the Forestry Minister and Kalimantan governors: fires burning for three months, a governor's joke when asked about karhutla, unpaid volunteer firefighters, and the claim that national TV ignores the story. On 16 Oct claims that the communications ministry was removing karhutla videos drew 32k likes, and the President reportedly called all ministers to the Bogor palace that evening. On 17 Oct posts reported that the KPK had arrested 14 people over alleged misuse of BNPB disaster funds (about Rp148bn, including a brother of a former BNPB head), money meant in part for karhutla.
  3. Health and daily hardship are the emotional core. ISPA counts, closed schools, children bathing in rivers for lack of clean water, and power cuts carry about a fifth of all engagement (ISPA and breathing alone, about a sixth). Ministry of Health figures cited on X rose from 174,694 ISPA cases (to 21 Sep) to 283,270 (to 13 Oct), and a 17 Oct post cited 311,363. On 15 Oct posts reported the death of a 14-month-old in Samarinda after breathing problems, and by 16 Oct four firefighters and volunteers had died fighting a fire at Bromo in East Java; the post mourning them drew 53k likes and 1.5M views.
  4. Corporate responsibility is moving up. Its share of engagement rose from 3.6% in the 16 Oct run to 6–9% in the 17 Oct runs (4.7% in the late 17 Oct sample), after Singapore's environment agency named seven Indonesian companies it links to the haze and WALHI released a report on firms and business people tied to the fires. 187 posts mention concessions or companies, citing WALHI's finding that 71–74% of Kalimantan hotspots fall in concessions and naming firms (Sinar Mas, Medco and others). All seven firms Singapore named hold pulpwood licences on this site's map; together they had 6,218 NASA fire detections on 9–16 Oct.
  5. Recovery is almost absent. “Recovery” on X means orangutan rescue (290 wild animals in care, 96 orangutans) and suspicion that burned land is being replanted with oil palm or acacia. Peat rewetting and restoration get few posts; one notable thread blames the dissolution of the peat restoration agency. Its successor, a national peat and mangrove agency, had its leaders sworn in on 15 Oct, which drew almost no engagement.
  6. The haze is a regional story. Malaysian and Singaporean users joke about “haze special effects” at F1, protest at the Indonesian embassy, and note Jakarta did not answer offers of help. On 15 Oct a Malaysian MP urged an ASEAN diplomatic protest, which Malaysia's foreign minister ruled out; on 16 Oct Singapore's prime minister said it would use its Transboundary Haze Pollution Act if needed. Indonesian users answer that Malaysian and Singaporean companies also hold burning concessions.
  7. Small personal accounts amplify the most. Many of the highest-engagement posts come from personal and fandom accounts with a few thousand followers, using all-caps “UP UP UP” calls to keep the topic trending. Big media accounts reach millions of followers but draw little engagement per post. On 17 Oct posts said streamer Windah Basudara was attacked in his comments as “paid” after speaking up about the fires.

    Method and limits

    How this was collected

    • Source: TwitterAPI.io advanced search, run 18 Oct 2026 (00:26 WIB), filtered to posts since 1 Aug 2026. Twelve queries covered core terms (karhutla, kebakaran hutan), English terms, hashtags, governance, enforcement, recovery, health and haze, firefighting response, transboundary haze, NGOs, and Papua / food estate.
    • Each query pulled up to three pages of “Top” and three of “Latest” results (about 120 posts per query), de-duplicated to 1,168 posts. Re-running replaces the sample, so counts shift from one run to the next.
    • This is a sample, not a census. “Top” favours viral posts, “Latest” favours the last two days. Reposts without comment are not included.
    • Themes come from keyword matching, not manual coding, so some posts are misclassified. Quotes are paraphrased; follow the links for full text.
    • Figures in posts are claims. Check them against KLHK/Kemenhut SiPongi, BNPB, Kemenkes and WALHI before reuse.

    Suggested next steps

    • Re-run the same queries weekly to track whether the October wave fades or turns into a sustained accountability campaign.
    • Cross-check the other companies named on X (Medco / PT Selaras Inti Semesta and others) against the concession and fire tables, as done here for the seven firms Singapore named.
    • Hand-code the top 200 posts for stance (blames government, companies, farmers, weather) to replace keyword shares with a firmer measure.
